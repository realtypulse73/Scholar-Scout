## User Constraints (from CONTEXT.md)

### Locked Decisions

### Adult invitation and self-attestation
- **D-01:** Staff grant a single, bounded contributor invitation to a known signed-in account. The private contributor route must not reveal submission capability, consent data, or asset metadata to signed-out, uninvited, disabled, or withdrawn accounts.
- **D-02:** Before a private draft exists, the invited contributor completes a plain-language, self-signing adult gate: they affirm that they are 18 or older, provide their signer name, and make a dated declaration of creator authority plus consent from every recognisable person. This is an adult self-attestation pilot, not age/identity verification and not the deferred parent/guardian flow for minors.
- **D-03:** The contributor starts with a compact purpose statement: the material is submitted for Scholar Scout's governed school-information workflow, not as an official Houston Tillotson University post, provider endorsement, attendance claim, placement claim, or promise of outcomes.

### Private single-package upload
- **D-04:** After the adult gate, the contributor may create one private package for one selected programme: exactly one original video of at most 30 seconds and one required still poster fallback. The private upload view validates file count, allowed type, duration, poster presence, and all required attestation fields before the package can proceed to review.
- **D-05:** Uploads remain private throughout draft and review. The server owns authorization and returns no learner-facing asset URL, private storage location, signer detail, or review evidence to the browser outside the contributor/reviewer views that require it.
- **D-06:** The contributor experience uses explicit private lifecycle feedback: `Draft`, `Ready for review`, `Action needed`, `Approved for release`, `Published`, and `Removed`. It supports a safe retry after an interrupted or failed upload without creating a second active package; detailed reviewer-only notes stay private while contributor-visible guidance is concise and actionable.

### Independent review, governed release, and withdrawal
- **D-07:** The Houston Tillotson association is a factual selected-school label, presented as non-endorsed. Submission, review approval, and governed publication are separate gates: the contributor cannot publish; a reviewer cannot perform the publisher's release action; and approval alone never exposes media to learners.
- **D-08:** Staff reuse the governed catalogue candidate and snapshot release boundary rather than creating a parallel public media channel. A reviewer validates the private attestation and content package; an authorized publisher subsequently decides whether the reviewed candidate enters the existing release flow.
- **D-09:** The contributor has a visible, repeat-safe `Remove my submission` control from every non-final private status. Removal immediately strips any public media projection, wins over a concurrent release attempt, leaves factual programme text and official sources intact, and requires a new invitation/submission/review for any future content.

### Public presentation and temporary illustration
- **D-10:** If a reviewed package is released, its learner-facing attribution is anonymous: use `Student-contributed perspective` rather than a name, account, profile, school relationship, or personal biography. Keep the quiet non-endorsement statement near the media, with factual source/actions still more important than the contribution.
- **D-11:** Any temporary synthetic still is an original, generic learning-environment illustration only. It must show the fixed disclosure `AI-generated illustration — not an official campus photograph`, never claim to depict Houston Tillotson University or another named school, and never use Google Maps, Street View, scraped/provider material, a real-campus near-replica, logos, seals, mascots, or readable signage as source/reference material.
- **D-12:** The temporary illustration is supporting detail-page context rather than the authoritative provider record or a simulated school photograph. It must remain visually and semantically distinct from the factual provider source, media label, and official verification actions.

### Choice and privacy boundary
- **D-13:** Invitation status, adult attestation, submissions, rights records, reviewer activity, release status, removal, and media interactions stay outside ranking, fit explanations, provider payloads, browser storage, public audit reasons, and new usage analytics. The pilot collects only the minimum private workflow data required to run the submission and review process.

## Summary

Use a separate, private Vercel Blob store for contributor binaries and a new private contributor-media domain state in the existing conditionally written ScholarScout document. Keep signer declarations, invitation status, private Blob pathnames, inspection output, reviewer-only notes, and removal evidence outside `CatalogueRenderableMedia`, public snapshots, public audit DTOs, browser storage, and analytics. The existing catalogue candidate/snapshot boundary remains the only release path; a released contribution adds an anonymous, narrow projection to the selected catalogue candidate, never a new feed or provider channel. [VERIFIED: codebase grep] [CITED: https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-blob/private-storage]

The technical hard point is duration validation. Vercel Functions limit request bodies to 4.5 MB, so a useful short-video pilot cannot safely rely on a server `FormData` upload. Use server-authorized Vercel Blob client upload to a private quarantine location, but do not treat the browser's `HTMLMediaElement.duration` result as release authority. The completion path must independently inspect the uploaded MP4 before `Ready for review`; the verified candidate is `mediainfo.js` [WARNING: flagged as suspicious — verify before using.] and requires a human verification/spike checkpoint before installation. [CITED: https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations] [CITED: https://mediainfo.js.org/docs/intro/]

**Primary recommendation:** Plan a small, server-owned contributor-media state machine plus private-Blob proxy delivery, add a human-verification Wave 0 for the metadata inspector and Vercel private store, then adapt the existing catalogue release/read boundary with an atomic revocation overlay.

## Architecture Patterns

### System Architecture Diagram

```text
Invited signed-in browser
  -> contributor page (local type/size/duration feedback)
  -> contributor upload-token route
      -> trusted session + invitation + current revision check
      -> Vercel private Blob client token
  -> private quarantine video + poster
  -> completion route
      -> Blob read + independent metadata inspection
      -> conditional write: Draft / Action needed / Ready for review

Active reviewer browser
  -> staff-only review route
      -> reviewer capability + separate reviewer != contributor check
      -> private attestation/package DTO only
      -> conditional write: Approved for release / Action needed

Active publisher browser
  -> existing catalogue candidate + snapshot release route
      -> administrator capability + current approved contribution revision
      -> anonymous public media projection only
      -> immutable reviewed snapshot
  -> learner `/programmes` / detail model
      -> approved snapshot + active, non-revoked projection
      -> `/api/catalogue-media/<opaque-public-id>` streaming route
      -> private Blob `get()`; no private pathname returned

Contributor removal
  -> trusted owner + current revision conditional mutation
      -> mark Removed, revoke public ID, delete/quarantine private objects per policy
      -> append media-removal snapshot/overlay before success
      -> provider facts and official source actions remain unchanged
```

### Pattern 1: Trusted actor plus conditionally written lifecycle

**What:** Resolve the account only with `resolveStudentActor({ allowGuest: false })`; look up the invitation by that trusted account ID; apply each invitation, draft, review, release, and removal transition through `commitConditionalMutation`. Return `409` on stale state, not a silent overwrite. [VERIFIED: `apps/web/lib/server/student-actor.ts`, `persistence-operations.ts`]

**When to use:** Every contributor or reviewer mutation, including an upload-completion callback. Do not accept an account ID, contributor ID, storage key, reviewer ID, status, or Blob pathname from the browser as authority. [VERIFIED: `student-actor.ts`, `active-staff.ts`]

**Example:**

```typescript
// Source: existing apps/web/lib/server/persistence-operations.ts pattern
const result = await commitConditionalMutation((data) => {
  const submission = findOwnedActiveSubmission(data, actor.accountId, input.id);
  if (!submission || submission.revision !== input.expectedRevision) {
    throw new ContributorMediaConflictError();
  }
  return removeSubmissionAndPublicProjection(data, submission, now);
});
if (result.status === 'conflict') throw new ContributorMediaConflictError();
```

### Pattern 2: Separate private record and public allowlist projection

**What:** Model private invitation/attestation/package/review data separately from catalogue candidate media. Add a pure function that returns only `{ publicId, kind, label, alt, disclosure }` for an approved, unrevoked contribution and returns `undefined` for every other status. Public API tests must assert forbidden fields are absent, not merely hidden in UI. [VERIFIED: `apps/web/lib/catalogue-publication.ts`, `apps/web/__tests__/lib/catalogue-discovery.test.ts`]

**When to use:** Catalogue release, snapshot hydration, learner detail/card rendering, and public streaming authorization.

### Pattern 3: Revocation wins over release

**What:** Re-check contribution lifecycle/revision inside the same conditional write that creates the release projection. Contributor removal changes status to `removed` and invalidates the public ID in a conditional write that also appends a media-removal snapshot or applies an active-revocation overlay before the route returns. A stale release must return conflict; it must never retry automatically. [VERIFIED: `apps/web/lib/server/catalogue-publications.ts`, `persistence-operations.ts`]

**When to use:** Approval/release/removal concurrency tests and all release helpers.

### Pattern 4: Blob is private until public projection proxy authorization

**What:** Use a Vercel private Blob store for both video/poster quarantine/review assets. Generate a client token only after actor/invitation/revision checks. The review endpoint streams an object only to its owner or authorized reviewer. The learner endpoint may stream a released object only after resolving the opaque public ID against the active released projection; it must not accept a Blob pathname query parameter. [CITED: https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-blob/private-storage]

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Direct `FormData` video upload to a Vercel route:** function payloads over 4.5 MB fail with 413. [CITED: https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations]
- **One `CatalogueRenderableMedia` object for consent and display:** it is intentionally serializable learner presentation data and contains public-facing source/asset fields. [VERIFIED: `apps/web/lib/catalogue-publication.ts`]
- **Making a private Blob URL the `<video>` source:** it exposes object location and cannot be read anonymously; proxy only deliberate released media by opaque ID. [CITED: https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-blob/private-storage]
- **Allowing reviewer approval to invoke normal publication:** existing weekly release already requires administrator capability; retain that split. [VERIFIED: `apps/web/app/api/admin/catalogue-publications/route.ts`]
- **Adding contributor events to platform analytics or discovery/recommendation models:** the phase explicitly forbids it and existing `platform-store` has a separate analytics collection. [VERIFIED: `13.2-CONTEXT.md`, `apps/web/lib/server/platform-store.ts`]

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Client upload callback marks a package ready without independent inspection

**What goes wrong:** A browser can misstate MIME, duration, or file identity, then a callback records a reviewable package.  
**Why it happens:** Vercel client upload is necessary for files larger than the Function body cap, which means the object arrives before ordinary route-side `FormData` validation. [CITED: https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-blob/client-upload] [CITED: https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations]  
**How to avoid:** Preflight in browser for good UX, then server-read the quarantined Blob and inspect type/container/duration before moving from `Draft`/`Action needed` to `Ready for review`; delete or retain only a non-reviewable quarantine record on failure.  
**Warning signs:** The completion route trusts a duration supplied by browser JSON, has no inspection result, or can enter `Ready for review` with an invalid Blob. [ASSUMED]

### Pitfall 2: Private evidence leaks through a seemingly harmless public field

**What goes wrong:** A Blob URL, signer name, reviewer note, or consent declaration appears in an API DTO, snapshot digest/audit, detail dialog, or browser storage.  
**Why it happens:** Existing media `assetPath` and `sourceUrl` are designed to be learner-visible. [VERIFIED: `CatalogueRenderableMedia`, `DiscoveryPreviewSlot.tsx`]  
**How to avoid:** Define negative-field tests for all learner JSON and `getPublishedCatalogueSnapshot`; public projection has only an opaque ID, anonymous label, generic accessible description, fixed non-endorsement/disclosure, and no direct Blob URL.  
**Warning signs:** Public component props mention account, signer, consent, reviewer, Blob pathname/URL, or a source called “contributor evidence.” [ASSUMED]

### Pitfall 3: Revocation updates private state but leaves a released snapshot renderable

**What goes wrong:** A contributor sees `Removed` while cached/public snapshot media keeps playing.  
**Why it happens:** Normal catalogue release produces immutable snapshots; revocation is a new immediate removal requirement. [VERIFIED: `catalogue-publications.ts`, `13.2-SPEC.md`]  
**How to avoid:** Make active public projection resolution consult a current, conditional revocation state or append a removal snapshot atomically; response success occurs only once learner model/proxy eligibility resolves to no media. Add race tests where release and remove begin from the same revision.  
**Warning signs:** Removal only deletes Blob bytes, only hides contributor UI, or waits for Monday weekly release. [ASSUMED]

### Pitfall 4: Houston Tillotson wording drifts into provider endorsement

**What goes wrong:** The selected-school label, contributor description, or temporary illustration implies an official relationship or represents a real campus.  
**Why it happens:** The first pilot has a named-school association but no provider clearance. [VERIFIED: `13.2-CONTEXT.md`, `13.2-SPEC.md`]  
**How to avoid:** Treat school association as a controlled factual programme selection; pin the exact non-endorsement and AI-illustration copy in pure constants/tests and require a human content/provenance checkpoint before release.  
**Warning signs:** “HTU campus,” a provider logo/seal/mascot/signage, source links to provider media, or an empty/mutable disclosure. [ASSUMED]
